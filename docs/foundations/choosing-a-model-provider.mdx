How do you pick among models and providers without getting lost in hype? With a simple, evergreen process — because the *specific* leaderboard changes monthly, but the *way to choose* doesn't.

## Read benchmarks skeptically

Public benchmark scores are a starting hint, not a verdict:

- They can be **gamed or contaminated** (test data leaking into training).
- They measure **generic** tasks, not *your* task.
- Small score gaps rarely matter in practice.

Use them to build a shortlist, not to make the final call.

## The only benchmark that counts: yours

Run a tiny **[eval](/docs/foundations/evals)** on a handful of *your real inputs* across 2–3 candidate models. It takes minutes and tells you what no leaderboard can. This "bake-off" is the single best habit in model selection.

<Steps items={[
  {title: "Shortlist 2–3 candidates", body: "Use public leaderboards, price lists and capability tables to narrow the field — and stop there. The shortlist is all a benchmark is good for."},
  {title: "Collect a handful of your real inputs", body: "Not synthetic examples. Pull actual prompts, documents or user messages from the workload you intend to run, including the awkward ones that currently fail."},
  {title: "Write down what 'good' means before you look at output", body: "Decide the pass condition in advance — correct extraction, no refusal, a parseable shape, under a latency budget. Judging after the fact is how a bake-off turns into a vibe check."},
  {title: "Run the same inputs through every candidate", body: "Same prompt, same inputs, one variable at a time. Keep the raw outputs so you can re-read them when you disagree with your own scoring."},
  {title: "Score, then decide on the scorecard below", body: "Quality is one column of seven. A model that wins the bake-off narrowly and loses badly on cost or latency is not the winner."},
  {title: "Re-run it when anything moves", body: "A new model, a price change, or a drift in your inputs invalidates the result. Keep the eval as a file you can re-run in minutes, not a one-off afternoon."},
]} />

<PromptCard title="Turn a handful of real inputs into a reusable bake-off spec">
{`I want to choose between these candidate models for one specific task: [list candidates].

The task: [describe it in one or two sentences].
Here are [N] real inputs from the workload, including [N] that currently fail: [paste inputs].

Help me build a small eval I can re-run:
1. Write the pass/fail condition for each input, in advance and in plain language.
2. Flag any input where "good" is ambiguous, and ask me to decide before we score.
3. Give me a single prompt to send to every candidate unchanged.
4. Propose a scoring table with one row per input and one column per candidate.
Do not rank the models yet — I will run them and bring the outputs back.`}
</PromptCard>

## A decision scorecard

Weigh what actually matters for your use case:

| Factor | Ask |
|---|---|
| **Quality on your task** | Does the bake-off show it's good enough? |
| **Cost** | Per-token price at your volume ([Cost & Latency](/docs/foundations/cost-and-latency)) |
| **Latency** | Fast enough for the experience? |
| **Capabilities** | Vision? Long context? Tool use? Structured output? |
| **Privacy/compliance** | Data handling, residency, certifications ([Privacy](/docs/foundations/privacy)) |
| **Reliability & ecosystem** | Uptime, SDKs, docs, support, migration story |
| **Lock-in** | How hard to switch later? |

## Practical posture

- **Default to a capable mid-tier model** and only move up/down on evidence.
- **Abstract the model behind config**, not scattered literals, so switching is a one-line change ([Errors & Migration](/docs/api/errors-and-rate-limits)).
- **Re-evaluate periodically** — the frontier moves fast; today's best may not be next quarter's.
