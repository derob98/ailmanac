Data-handling and training policies differ by provider, product, and plan (consumer vs API vs enterprise) and change over time. Nothing on this page is a substitute for reading your own provider's current terms — treat it as the list of questions to ask, not the answers.
</VerifyNote>

A simple question with real stakes: **what happens to the text you send an AI?** The answer depends on the provider, the product, and your plan — so the safe habit is to *know before you paste*.

The most common mistake is assuming the guarantee follows the **subject matter**. It almost never does. Guarantees attach to a **surface** (this feature, this hub, this API) or to a **plan** (enterprise, zero-retention) — which means the same sentence about your salary can be handled two completely different ways depending on where in the same app you typed it.

## Four questions, not one

"Is it private?" collapses four independent properties. A tool can score well on one and badly on another, so ask them separately.

<Steps items={[
  {title: "Training — is my input used to improve the model?", body: "This varies enormously by tier. Many API and enterprise offerings do NOT train on your data by default; consumer settings often depend on a toggle you may or may not have changed. Check the actual setting in your account, not the marketing page."},
  {title: "Retention — how long is it kept, and does deleting really delete?", body: "Removing a conversation from your history and removing it from back-end storage are two different events with two different timelines. Ask for both numbers. Ask separately about data flagged for policy review, which is usually retained far longer."},
  {title: "Access — who inside the provider can read it, and under what trigger?", body: "Safety review, abuse investigation, and support escalation are normal parts of running a service. The question is not whether humans can ever see it, but what causes them to."},
  {title: "Residency — where is it processed, and is it encrypted?", body: "Matters for GDPR, sector rules, and customer contracts. If a specific region is required, that is usually an enterprise-tier feature, not a consumer setting."},
  {title: "Then: is there a zero-retention or business option at all?", body: "If sensitive work is routine for you, the answer to the previous four questions should be 'we are on the tier where this is contractual' rather than 'we checked the toggle.'"}

<PromptCard title="Paste this into any AI vendor's docs search, or send it to their sales team">
{`For the specific product and plan we would be on:
1. Is customer input used for model training by default? Is it a per-user or
   per-organization setting, and who can change it?
2. What are the two retention windows — removal from the user-visible history,
   and deletion from back-end storage? What is retained longer, and why?
3. Under what triggers can your staff read customer conversations?
4. Can processing be pinned to a specific region, and on which plan?
5. Do you offer a zero-retention mode, and what does it disable?
Please point to the documentation for each answer rather than summarizing.`}
</PromptCard>

If a vendor cannot answer all five with links, that is itself the answer.

## The guarantee follows the surface, not the topic

The clearest live example is Anthropic's **Personal Finance** feature. Conversations inside the Personal Finance hub are not used to train Anthropic's models, and are kept out of memory and your monthly recap. Paste the same bank statement into an ordinary Claude chat and your **normal account privacy settings** apply instead — the exclusion travels with the hub, not with the fact that you were talking about money.

<Callout type="warning" items={[
  "Generalize the lesson, not the example: when you read a privacy promise, find the noun it is scoped to. 'In this workspace', 'on this plan', 'through this API', 'in this hub' — that noun is the actual boundary. A promise with no scope noun is usually a promise about the vendor's intentions rather than about your data."
]} />

## Practical rules of thumb

- **Don't paste secrets** — API keys, passwords, tokens — into any AI tool. Ever. Not because the vendor is untrustworthy, but because a secret in a transcript is a secret in a log, a backup, a shared link, and possibly a support ticket.
- **Minimize personal data.** Send only what the task needs; redact names, IDs, and account numbers when you can. A model rarely needs the identifier to do the reasoning.
- **Other people's data is their data.** Be careful with customer PII, health, or financial information — there may be legal obligations (GDPR, HIPAA, and others) that your personal comfort level does not override.
- **Use the right tier.** For regulated or confidential work, use a business/enterprise/zero-retention option, not a personal account. This is the single highest-leverage move on this page.
- **Consider local/self-hosted models** when data truly cannot leave your environment — you trade some capability for full control. See [a private local AI stack](/docs/models/private-local-ai-stack).

## Three surfaces people forget

Your prompt is the obvious one. These three leak in ways that surprise people.

| Surface | Why it is easy to miss | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Connected accounts** (banks, email, drive, CRM) | The grant lives at the *aggregator or OAuth provider*, not in the AI app. Cancelling your subscription or deleting the app often leaves the connection alive. | Audit and revoke at the source — the aggregator's portal, your Google/Microsoft account's third-party app list. Treat the AI app's own toggle as a convenience. See [Connectors](/docs/claude-app/connectors). |
| **Memory** | It is designed to carry context *across* conversations, which is exactly what makes it carry a disclosure you made once into chats where you did not expect it. | Know whether your tool has it, what it has stored, and how to clear a single entry rather than all of it. See [Memory Across Chats](/docs/claude-app/memory). |
| **Anything published or shared** | A shared link or a published artifact is frequently a *separate object* with its own lifetime — it can outlive the conversation it came from, and an interactive one can read the viewer's own connected data. | Before you share, ask what the recipient's copy can do, not just what it shows. Deleting the chat is often not a cleanup. |

## On-device vs cloud

| | Cloud (hosted) | Local / self-hosted |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | Highest | Lower (but improving) |
| Data leaves your machine | Yes (per provider terms) | No |
| Setup/cost | Easy, pay per use | More effort, your hardware |
| Who you have to trust | The provider's terms and controls | Your own machine, patching, and backups |

Local is not automatically more private — it is *differently* private. An unencrypted laptop with two years of transcripts on it is a worse outcome than a reputable provider on a zero-retention tier. Pick local when the requirement is genuinely "this must not leave the network," not as a reflex.
