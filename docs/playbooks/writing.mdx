AI is a phenomenal writing partner *if* you direct it. The goal isn't to push a button and publish — it's to compress the blank-page-to-strong-draft loop, then sharpen with your own judgment. This page is the workflow, the prompts, and the traps.

## The workflow that works

Six steps. Skip any of them and quality drops sharply.

<Steps items={[

## Prompts that work

### The brief-and-voice starter

<PromptCard title="First-draft brief with voice sample">
```text
Write a {FORMAT} of {LENGTH} for {AUDIENCE}.
Purpose: {ONE-SENTENCE GOAL}.
Tone: {THREE ADJECTIVES}. Avoid: {LIST}.

Here are 300 words I wrote — match this voice. Same sentence rhythm,
same vocabulary, same restraint. Do not "improve" it toward a
generic professional voice.

<voice_sample>
{PASTE A PARAGRAPH OR TWO YOU WROTE}
</voice_sample>

Mark every factual claim, statistic, quote, or attributed source
with [verify] at the end of the sentence, so I can check it before
publishing.
```
</PromptCard>

### The single-purpose edit

<PromptCard title="Editing pass with visible diff">
```text
Edit this text for {ONE GOAL — pick one: clarity, concision, concreteness, rhythm, cutting jargon}.
Do not change the meaning or reorder arguments.
After the edited version, list every change as a bullet: "removed X",
"replaced Y with Z", "cut sentence about W".

<text>
{PASTE}
</text>
```
</PromptCard>

Ask for the change list. It forces the model to make deliberate edits rather than silently rewriting to its own taste — and it lets you veto any change you disagree with.

### The outline-first for long-form

<PromptCard title="Outline before prose">
```text
I'm writing a {2000-word essay} on {topic} for {audience}.
The argument is {one-sentence thesis}.

Before drafting, give me an outline as bullets: section headings,
one line of substance per section, plus the transition between
sections. No prose yet. Aim for {N} sections.

I'll approve or edit the outline; then you draft.
```
</PromptCard>

### The repurposing chain

<PromptCard title="From one piece to five formats — without flattening">
```text
Here's a published piece: <source>{PASTE}</source>.

Repurpose it into:
1. A LinkedIn post (~150 words) — one specific insight, not a summary.
2. A Twitter/X thread (5–7 tweets) — start with the strongest hook, one idea per tweet.
3. A newsletter blurb (~80 words) — the "why this matters" angle for busy readers.
4. Three email subject lines that would make me open it.

Rules:
- Do NOT summarize. Each format should stand alone.
- Preserve my voice (match the source's rhythm and vocabulary).
- Different hook for each — no reused sentences.
```
</PromptCard>

## Voice matching — the mechanics

"Match my voice" is worthless without a sample. What actually works:

- **Paste real work, not descriptions.** "Confident but casual" is fuzzy; 400 words of your writing is not.
- **Pick a sample close to the target task.** Voice shifts between formats — your newsletter voice is not your LinkedIn voice.
- **Reject flattening explicitly.** Add: *"Do not smooth this toward a generic professional voice. Keep the fragments, the aside asides, the unusual word choices."*
- **Iterate on tone knobs.** After the first draft: *"Half a step more direct. Less hedging. Keep the sentence rhythm."*

If a model still writes in AI-voice after this, either the sample was too short, the request was too generic, or you're on a model tuned toward the mean — try [Fable 5 or Sonnet 5](/docs/models/sonnet-5-field-guide) which handle voice-imitation better than default GPT-family styles.

## The traps that make writers reject AI

<PromptCard title="Trap 1 — Fabricated facts, plausibly delivered">
The model invents statistics, quotes, source names, book titles, and even URLs. Confidently. Every one of them.

**Fix:** Force a `[verify]` marker on every factual claim. Never publish an unverified stat or quote. See [Hallucinations](/docs/foundations/hallucinations) for the four levels and how to detect each.
</PromptCard>

<PromptCard title="Trap 2 — Generic AI voice">
Balanced sentences. "Not only… but also…". "It's worth noting that…". "In today's fast-paced world". You know it when you see it.

**Fix:** Voice sample + explicit rejection of smoothing. If it slips back mid-draft, paste the offending paragraph back and say "rewrite this specific paragraph in the voice sample I gave you".
</PromptCard>

<PromptCard title="Trap 3 — Over-editing into mush">
Ten small "make it better" passes flatten the piece toward the mean of the model's training data. You started with a distinctive draft and ended with a competent, unmemorable one.

**Fix:** Single-purpose passes only. When in doubt, stop editing.
</PromptCard>

<PromptCard title="Trap 4 — The em-dash / bullet tell">
Excessive em-dashes, three-bullet lists after every claim, "however" between contrasting sentences. Readers now spot this instantly and discount the piece.

**Fix:** Explicit stylistic constraints — "max two em-dashes per section", "prose only, no bullets in this piece". Or write the framing sentences yourself and only ask the model for the middle.
</PromptCard>

<PromptCard title="Trap 5 — 'Rewrite this' loops">
You keep asking "make it stronger" and the model produces different mediocre drafts because you haven't told it what "stronger" means. Rounds of this waste hours.

**Fix:** Name the specific weakness. "The third paragraph is too abstract — give me a concrete example from a real 2020s startup." "The opening is throat-clearing — start with the punchline."
</PromptCard>

<PromptCard title="Trap 6 — Publishing without human editing">
The model wrote a "good enough" draft. You skimmed and published. Two weeks later you notice the awkward transition, the misused word, the paragraph that says nothing.

**Fix:** Print or read aloud before publishing. AI cannot hear rhythm the way you can.
</PromptCard>

## Model choice for writing tasks (2026)

| Task | Best default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form essay, voice-matched | Claude Sonnet 5 or [Fable 5](/docs/models/fable-5-mythos-5-flagship) | Strongest at rhythm, restraint, and voice imitation |
| Fast blog draft, workmanlike prose | Claude Sonnet 5 or GPT-5 | Fast, competent, cheap |
| Poetry, narrative fiction, distinctive style | Fable 5, then hand-edit | Better at unusual voice targets |
| SEO/marketing copy with keyword targets | Any frontier model + explicit constraints | All roughly equivalent; the brief matters more than the model |
| Editing existing prose | Sonnet 5 or Opus 4 | Best at surgical single-purpose edits |
| Translation / localization | See [Porting Prompts Across Models](/docs/models/porting-prompts-across-models) | Voice preservation across languages is a distinct skill |

## Ethics and disclosure

- **You are the editor.** Judgment, taste, and accountability stay with you.
- **Disclose AI assistance where it matters** — journalism, academia, contracted work with disclosure clauses. Increasingly, readers assume AI involvement in *everything*; being explicit about what YOU brought builds trust.
- **Do not pass off fabricated quotes or statistics as researched.** This has already ended careers in 2024–2025.
