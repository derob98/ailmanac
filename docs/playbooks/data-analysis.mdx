You don't need to be a data scientist to get answers from data with AI — but you do need to keep it honest. AI writes the query and explains the result; **you** confirm the numbers are real.

## What it helps with

- **Write SQL / spreadsheet formulas / pandas** from a plain-language question.
- **Explain** what a query or result means in business terms.
- **Clean & transform** messy data (describe the mess, get steps).
- **Suggest the right chart** for the question.
- **Interpret** output and propose the next question.

## The safe workflow

<Steps items={[
  {title: "Describe your data", body: "Columns, types, and the GRAIN — one row equals what, exactly? Don't assume the model can guess your names or that order_id is unique. Most wrong queries are wrong about the grain, not the SQL."},
  {title: "State the question the analysis should answer", body: "In business terms, with the time range and the filter spelled out. 'Monthly revenue from completed orders in 2025' is answerable; 'how are sales doing' is not."},
  {title: "Get the query plus a one-line explanation of what it does and its assumptions", body: "The explanation is the part you review. If the stated assumptions don't match your data, the query is wrong no matter how clean it looks."},
  {title: "Run it yourself on the real data", body: "This is the non-negotiable step. A model that reports a result without executing against your data is guessing at a plausible number."},
  {title: "Bring the results back for plain-language interpretation and caveats", body: "Paste the actual output. Now the model is reading numbers instead of inventing them, which is the one thing it is reliably good at here."}
]} />

## Guardrails

:::warning Never trust numbers it didn't compute
If the AI states a result without running the query on your data, that number may be **invented**. Run queries yourself; treat any figure you didn't compute as unverified. And remember **correlation ≠ causation** — ask it to flag associational claims. See [Hallucinations](/docs/foundations/hallucinations).
:::

- **Sanity-check magnitudes** — implausible result? investigate, don't publish.
- **State assumptions** (nulls, dedup, time zones, currency).
- **Don't paste sensitive/PII data** into tools you haven't vetted ([Privacy](/docs/foundations/privacy)).

Those four assumptions are listed in a specific order because that is roughly how often they bite. Nulls silently shrink a denominator; duplicate rows silently inflate a sum; a timestamp stored in UTC and read as local time moves revenue across a month boundary; and a `total` column that mixes currencies produces a number that is arithmetically correct and economically meaningless.

<Callout type="tip" items={["Ask for the query AND a second query that sanity-checks it — a row count, a date range, a sum you can eyeball. Two cheap queries beat one elegant one you cannot verify.","When a result surprises you, suspect the join before you suspect the business. A fan-out join is the most common source of a number that is suddenly 3× too big.","For spreadsheet work, ask for the formula plus one worked example cell. If the example is wrong, you have found the bug without touching your real file."]} />
