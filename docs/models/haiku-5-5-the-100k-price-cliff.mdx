<LevelBadge level="intermediate" />

On **7 October 2026** Anthropic released **Claude Haiku 5.5** (`claude-haiku-5-5`): the fastest
model in the lineup, with a **1M context window** (up from 200K), **128K max output** (up from
64K), adaptive thinking with the `effort` parameter for the first time in a Haiku, and a headline
input price of **$0.10 / MTok** against Haiku 4.5's $1.00.

That is a 90% cut on the rate card, and it is the number every write-up led with. It is also not
the number you will pay, for two reasons that compound — and one of them is a structural change
nothing else in the lineup has.

**Haiku 5.5 is the only current Claude model priced by prompt length.** Every other Claude 4.6-and-later
model bills a 900K-token request at exactly the same per-token rate as a 9K-token one. Haiku 5.5
has two rate cards, and the switch between them is a **hard threshold at 100,000 tokens** that
reprices *the whole request* — input, output, cache writes, cache reads, batch — at **5x**.

The second reason is quieter: Haiku 5.5 moves to the newer tokenizer, so **the same text counts as
about 30% more tokens** than it did on Haiku 4.5. That shrinks the discount, and it also moves the
cliff: the 100,000 is counted in *new* tokens, so it arrives roughly 23% earlier in terms of actual
text than anyone carrying over a Haiku 4.5 measurement will assume.

This page works out what you actually pay in each regime, shows where the cliff sits in words, and
then covers the migration — including five 400-errors and six changes that return `200` and simply
give you a worse answer.

Three of those rows deserve more than a glance.

**The two input rows are the story.** No other current Claude model has two. Anthropic's long-context
pricing section states the exception explicitly: Claude 4.6 and later models *except Haiku 5.5*
include the full 1M window at standard pricing. Haiku 5.5 is "priced by prompt length."

**Haiku 5.5 is the most permissive current model on determinism.** It accepts `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}`
(at `low`, `medium` or `high` effort) *and* a forced `tool_choice`. Opus 5.5 rejects both. Sonnet 5.5
rejects both. If the 5.5 generation broke a deterministic classifier you run on Haiku 4.5, Haiku 5.5
is the one model in the family you can move it to largely intact — you just lose prefill, which is
covered below.

**Haiku 4.5's floor is 15 October 2026 — one week after Haiku 5.5 shipped.** The official status
table still lists it **Active** with no deprecation announced, so this is not an outage warning; a
"not sooner than" date passing is a signal to plan, and Anthropic commits to at least 60 days'
notice once a notice goes out. But it is now the nearest floor in the lineup, and it now has a
direct successor. See [deprecations](/docs/whats-new/deprecations) for how to read that table.

## The cliff is a repricing, not an overage

This is the single most important mechanical fact on the page, and it is the one most likely to be
assumed away. The 100,000-token threshold does **not** work like a phone plan, where you pay the
cheap rate up to the limit and a higher rate on the excess. Anthropic's rate card is written per
*prompt*: one row "for prompts up to 100,000 tokens," another "for prompts over 100,000 tokens."
Cross the line and **every token in that request** is billed at the higher rate.

Take a 500-token answer and walk one token across the boundary:

| Request | Input cost | Output cost | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100,000-token prompt | 100,000 × $0.10/M = **$0.0100** | 500 × $0.50/M = $0.00025 | **$0.01025** |
| 100,001-token prompt | 100,001 × $0.50/M = **$0.0500** | 500 × $2.50/M = $0.00125 | **$0.05125** |

One extra token, exactly **5x** the bill. There is no gradient and no blend. A workload whose
prompts cluster around 100K is a workload whose monthly bill has a step function in it, and which
side of the step you land on may depend on a user pasting one more paragraph.

The practical consequence: **treat 100,000 as a hard budget, not a target.** Leave headroom. A
pipeline designed to run at 98K tokens has a 2% margin before its cost quintuples, and the things
that quietly grow a prompt — a longer retrieved chunk, an extra few-shot example, a tool definition
you added last week, conversation history — do not announce themselves.

## Where the cliff actually sits, in text

The threshold is counted in Haiku 5.5 tokens, on the new tokenizer. Two conversions matter.

**In words.** Anthropic puts 1M tokens at roughly 555K English words on the current tokenizer. So
100,000 tokens is **about 55,500 words** — call it a 180-page book, or 20-25 substantial PDFs.

**In old measurements.** The same text produces about 30% more tokens on Haiku 5.5 than on Haiku
4.5. So a prompt you measured at 100,000 tokens on Haiku 4.5 arrives at roughly **130,000** tokens
on Haiku 5.5 — comfortably over the cliff, at 5x, on text that used to be exactly at your limit.
Running it backwards: to stay under 100,000 new tokens you need about **77,000 Haiku 4.5 tokens**
worth of text.

That 23% shrinkage is the trap. Anyone who sizes a Haiku 5.5 prompt from a Haiku 4.5 token count —
a cached measurement, a stored `usage` value, a spreadsheet, a hard-coded constant in a chunker —
will under-measure by about a third and cross a threshold they believe they are well inside.

<Callout type="warning">
Recount every prompt with `model` set to `claude-haiku-5-5`. The token-counting endpoint is
tokenizer-specific; a count taken on Haiku 4.5 is not an estimate of the Haiku 5.5 count, it is a
different measurement of a different thing. This applies to `max_tokens` values and cost models too.
</Callout>

## The honest saving: three regimes

"90% cheaper" is a rate-card statement. What you pay depends on which band you land in and on the
tokenizer. All three numbers below hold the *text* constant and let the token count move, which is
the only comparison that means anything to a bill.

### Below the cliff: ~87% less than Haiku 4.5

A classification workload, 1,000,000 requests/month. The prompt measures 9,000 tokens on Haiku 4.5,
so about 11,700 on Haiku 5.5; the reply is 300 old tokens, about 390 new.

| | Haiku 4.5 | Haiku 5.5 (≤ 100K) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input | 9,000 MTok × $1 = $9,000 | 11,700 MTok × $0.10 = **$1,170** |
| Output | 300 MTok × $5 = $1,500 | 390 MTok × $0.50 = **$195** |
| **Total** | **$10,500** | **$1,365** |

**87% less** for the same work. The 30% token inflation eats three points of the 90% headline. That
is the number to put in a budget.

### Above the cliff: ~35% less than Haiku 4.5

Above 100,000 tokens the rates are $0.50/$2.50 against Haiku 4.5's $1/$5 — 2x cheaper per token.
Apply the 30% token inflation and the saving on the same text is `1 − (0.5 × 1.3) = 0.35`.

**35% less**, uniformly, on input and output alike. Still a cut, nothing like the headline. A
long-context workload that migrates from Haiku 4.5 expecting a 90% drop will miss by a factor of
nearly three.

### Either way: 75% less than Sonnet 5.5

Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 share the newer tokenizer, so the token counts are identical and the
comparison is pure price. Above the cliff, $0.50/$2.50 against $2/$10 is exactly **4x cheaper**, or
75% less. Below the cliff it is 20x.

This is the reassuring row: even in its expensive band, Haiku 5.5 is substantially the cheapest
current Claude model. The cliff costs you the headline, not the tier.

## When to chunk below the threshold — and when not to

If the cliff reprices whole requests, splitting one long request into several short ones is a real
lever. It is also frequently the wrong call. Here is the arithmetic, then the limits.

A batch of 1,000 documents, each 150,000 tokens in and 2,000 tokens out:

| Strategy | Input | Output | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 whole documents (> 100K, 5x rates) | 150 MTok × $0.50 = $75.00 | 2 MTok × $2.50 = $5.00 | **$80.00** |
| 2,000 halves of 75K (≤ 100K rates) | 150 MTok × $0.10 = $15.00 | 4 MTok × $0.50 = $2.00 | **$17.00** |

**79% less, on identical input tokens.** The chunked run even pays for *double* the output — two
replies per document instead of one — and still wins by a factor of nearly five, because the rate
difference swamps the duplication.

## The cache-read trap

Prompt caching is the usual answer to an expensive prefix, and on Haiku 5.5 it interacts with the
cliff in a way that is easy to miss: the rate card prices **every** token category in both bands.
Cache writes and cache reads each have a cheap-band price and an expensive-band price. Caching does
not exempt you from the threshold; it gets repriced alongside everything else.

An agent with a 120,000-token cached prefix on the 1-hour cache, one write and 100 reads:

| Strategy | Cache write | 100 cache reads | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 120K prefix (> 100K: $1 write, $0.05 read) | 0.12 MTok × $1 = $0.120 | 12 MTok × $0.05 = $0.600 | **$0.720** |
| 95K prefix (≤ 100K: $0.20 write, $0.01 read) | 0.095 MTok × $0.20 = $0.019 | 9.5 MTok × $0.01 = $0.095 | **$0.114** |

Cutting **21%** of the prefix cuts **84%** of the cost. There is no other lever in Claude pricing
with that shape — on every other current model, trimming 21% of a prompt saves 21%.

<Callout type="warning">
The band is selected by prompt length, and a cached prefix is part of the prompt. The safe
engineering assumption is that **total request input — cached tokens included — picks the rate
card**, which means a cache that grows past 100,000 tokens takes your reads from $0.01 to $0.05
with no code change and no error. Verify against the `usage` block on your own requests and the
Console's cost breakdown for your exact shape before you commit a budget to it.
</Callout>

One more caching interaction, from the prompting guide: **changing the top-level `effort` value
between requests invalidates the prompt cache** for the conversation's messages. To vary effort per
turn without destroying the cache, use the per-message effort change (beta, `mid-conversation-output-config-2026-07-01`),
which requires adaptive thinking — with thinking off it returns a 400.

## Migrating from Haiku 4.5: the five 400s

Anthropic's own line is that existing Haiku 4.5 *prompts* should perform well unchanged. The
*requests* are a different matter. Five things that Haiku 4.5 accepted now fail closed.

| What you send | Result | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `thinking: {"type": "enabled", "budget_tokens": N}` | 400 | Send `{"type": "adaptive"}` (or omit) and steer with `effort` |
| `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}` at `xhigh` or `max` effort | 400 | Fine at `low`/`medium`/`high`; lower the effort or drop the field |
| Non-default `temperature` / `top_p`, any `top_k`, or both `temperature` and `top_p` together | 400 | Omit all three. If present, `temperature` must be `1` and `top_p` must be `0.99` — `top_p: 1` is a 400 |
| A final assistant turn for the model to continue (prefill) | 400, **even with thinking off** | End `messages` with a user turn; see below |
| `computer_20250124` on the Claude API or Google Cloud | 400 | Use `{"type": "computer_toolset_20260801"}`, drop the `computer-use-2025-01-24` header |

Plus a sixth that only bites conversations: sending a thinking block back after you changed
`system`, `tools` or any earlier message returns a 400. Keep conversations **append-only**. On
accounts created before 31 August 2026 00:00 UTC the error fires only when you set
`thinking.block_binding.prefix_mismatch_behavior`.

### Prefill is the one that will hurt

Assistant prefill — ending `messages` with a partial assistant turn like `{"label":` to force a
shape — is a standard trick in exactly the high-volume classification and extraction pipelines
Haiku is built for, and Haiku 4.5 accepted it with thinking off. Haiku 5.5 returns a 400 with
thinking off too. There is no flag.

Replace it by what it was doing:

- **Forcing an output shape** → [structured outputs](/docs/api/structured-output), or a tool with
  `enum` fields for classification. (On Amazon Bedrock, which does not support structured outputs,
  use the tool.)
- **Suppressing a preamble** → ask for a direct answer in the system prompt.
- **Continuing a truncated reply** → move it into the user turn: *"Your previous response was
  interrupted and ended with `[previous_response]`. Continue from where you left off."*
- **Reminding the model of context** → put it in the user turn.

<Callout type="note">
Keep in mind the compensation: Haiku 5.5 still accepts a **forced `tool_choice`**, which Opus 5.5
and Sonnet 5.5 reject. For a classifier, `tool_choice` with an `enum`-typed tool gets you the same
determinism prefill gave you, and it survives the whole 5.5 generation. The one cost is that the
response starts with the tool call and contains no thinking block.
</Callout>

## The six changes that return 200

The 400s announce themselves in your logs on the first request. These do not. Each one returns a
perfectly valid response that is quietly worse, and each has broken someone's pipeline.

1. **An empty reply from a `max_tokens` you did not change.** Thinking is on by default and
   thinking tokens count toward `max_tokens`. A limit sized for Haiku 4.5 running *without*
   thinking can now stop with `stop_reason: "max_tokens"` after a thinking block and before any
   text. Raise `max_tokens`, or lower `effort`.
2. **`content[0]` is no longer the answer.** Responses can begin with one or more `thinking` blocks
   even when the request never mentions thinking. Select blocks by their `type` field, never by
   position.
3. **Thinking text is empty by default.** Haiku 4.5 returned summarized thinking; Haiku 5.5 returns
   each block with an empty `thinking` field and only a `signature`. If you log or display
   reasoning, set `thinking.display` to `"summarized"` to get it back.
4. **Refusals are new, and there is no safety net.** Haiku 5.5 runs safety classifiers that can
   return `stop_reason: "refusal"` with a `stop_details.category` of `cyber`, `frontier_llm`, `bio`
   or `general_harms`. Haiku 4.5 did not do this. Haiku 5.5 also has **no server-side fallback** —
   unlike Sonnet 5.5, nothing retries for you, and resending the same request usually earns another
   refusal. An unhandled refusal looks exactly like an empty response. Handle it in the client.
5. **Cross-account thinking replay silently drops reasoning.** Haiku 5.5 thinking blocks work only
   in the account that produced them (or a linked one). Send one from another account and the API
   *removes the block and succeeds without it* — a quality regression with no error. If you store
   conversations and replay them, replay each through its producing account.
6. **Every token number you have is now wrong by ~30%.** `usage` fields, token-counting results,
   `max_tokens`, cost models, chunk sizes, context budgets, and — as covered above — which side of
   the 100,000-token cliff you are on.

And one capacity item that is neither: if you hold a **Priority Tier** commitment on Haiku 4.5,
plan separately. Priority Tier is not supported on Haiku 5.5.

## Effort: the new dial, and what it costs

Haiku 5.5 is the first Haiku with `effort`, and it replaces `budget_tokens` outright — there is no
old setting to carry over. Anthropic's guidance by level:

| Level | Use it for | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `low` | Chat, short tool tasks, simple high-volume requests | In long agent prompts: skipped searches, early stopping, skipped checks |
| `medium` | **The default.** Most work, including agentic coding | — |
| `high` | Knowledge work, longer agent tasks, strict instruction following | — |
| `xhigh` / `max` | Only when your evals show the quality gain pays | Thinking and replies get much longer; also benchmark Sonnet 5.5 at this point |

Two findings from Anthropic's own testing are worth internalizing, because both contradict an
intuition.

**You cannot prompt your way out of thinking.** Telling the model in the prompt to answer directly
did not stop it from thinking. Lower the effort level, or set `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}` (at
`high` or below). Prose is not the lever.

**Raising effort is not a free fix.** Moving `low` → `medium` roughly halved early stopping in a
long coding-agent prompt — and *more than doubled* the output tokens per attempt. Both halves of
that sentence belong in the decision.

A third, narrower one: at `xhigh` effort in multi-turn chats the model sometimes writes its whole
answer inside its thinking and ends the turn with no visible text. If you run `xhigh`, check for
empty replies.

## Two prompt additions that earn their tokens

The prompting guide is unusually concrete about which system-prompt text moved which metric. Two
are worth copying as-is if you hit the matching symptom.

If you give Haiku 5.5 a search tool, **give it today's date** — Anthropic's testing found this alone
grounded answers in recent results. At `low` effort or behind a long system prompt it may still need
a nudge to search at all:

<PromptCard title="Nudge an under-searching Haiku 5.5 (place directly after the date)">
{`The current date is [current_date].

Your training data ends well before today's date. Records, office holders, prices, versions, rules and anything "latest" may have changed since then, so search for those before you answer, even when you feel sure. Facts that can't change need no search. When the answer depends on where the user is, put the user's country or region in the search query.`}
</PromptCard>

Anthropic reports this raised the search rate on questions whose answers had changed, while adding
searches on only 0-3% of prompts that needed none. The explicit anti-pattern: do **not** write
"search for any present-day factual question, regardless of how confident you are" — that made the
model search on half of the prompts that needed no search, without producing more correct answers.

The second applies to a specific, nasty combination: **thinking off plus structured outputs**, where
the model may skip a tool call it needs. The clean fixes are to use adaptive thinking for those
requests, drop `output_config.format` where a tool call is required, or force the call with
`tool_choice`. If you genuinely need thinking off:

<PromptCard title="Keep tool calls alive with thinking off and JSON output">
{`The JSON output format applies to your final answer only. When you need a tool, call it first, with no text before the call, and write the JSON once you have the results.`}
</PromptCard>

## Should you move?

A short decision rule, in order.

1. **Prompts reliably under ~85,000 new tokens?** Move. ~87% less than Haiku 4.5 for the same work,
   with a 1M window and a June 2026 cutoff instead of Feb 2025. This is most Haiku workloads —
   classification, routing, extraction, cheap subagents — and it is close to a free win once the
   five 400s are fixed.
2. **Prompts straddling 100,000?** Measure first, then trim or chunk. The step function is the
   whole problem; getting under the line is worth more than any other optimization available to you.
3. **Prompts genuinely over 100,000 and not separable?** Still move — it is 35% less than Haiku 4.5
   and 75% less than Sonnet 5.5 — but budget from the high band and ignore the headline entirely.
4. **Need `budget_tokens`, prefill, Priority Tier, or `computer_20250124`?** Those are gone. The
   migration is mechanical for the first three; price the work before you schedule it.
5. **Running near the quality ceiling already?** Benchmark Sonnet 5.5 in the same sweep. Anthropic
   recommends it explicitly once you are reaching for `xhigh` or `max` on Haiku — at that point you
   may be paying Haiku prices for Sonnet-shaped work and getting neither.
