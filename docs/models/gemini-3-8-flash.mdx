Google shipped **Gemini 3.8 Flash** on **September 2, 2026**, twenty days after [3.7 Flash](/docs/models/gemini-3-7-flash) and its **third Flash release in six weeks**. Same tier, same 1M window, same $0.75 / $3.75 introductory sticker, same model-ID pattern. What changed is the shape of the model's behavior: Google says 3.8 Flash "works harder," executing extra reasoning steps and calling tools iteratively on complex tasks, and the benchmark table shows what that buys. **DeepSWE v1.1 moved from 65.3 to 73.7**, within a third of a point of Claude Opus 5. **Terminal-bench 2.1 reached 89.4**, a hair above Opus 5's 89.1. And on **Terminal-bench 4.0**, the harder successor, 3.8 Flash posts **19.1 against Opus 5's 51.8**.

This lesson is for a Claude user deciding whether to route work to it. It covers the numbers that are load-bearing, the token-usage cost of "working harder," the migration checklist that has not changed since 3.7, the gated Cyber variant, and the workloads where Claude is still the right default.

## The one-sentence version

Gemini 3.8 Flash is the same-tier successor to 3.7 Flash, model ID **`gemini-3.8-flash`**, **1M-token context**, **64K max output**, `thinking_level` of `low` / `medium` (default) / `high`, priced at **$0.75 in / $3.75 out per 1M tokens through December 31, 2026** and **$1.50 / $7.50 from January 1, 2027**. It is live in the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Antigravity, Android Studio, Stitch, Gemini Enterprise, the Gemini app (AI Pro and Ultra), AI Mode in Search, and Google Sheets. Alongside it, **Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber** replaces 3.5 Flash Cyber for vetted defenders through the new **Fairwind Program**.

The cadence is the story again. 3.6 Flash on July 21, 3.7 on August 13, 3.8 on September 2: Google is iterating its workhorse tier every three weeks while the flagship stays quiet (The Register notes 3.5 Pro missed its June date). Read that as a fast developer feedback loop at the tier most teams actually pay for. Anthropic's answer this month is [Claude Fable 5.1](/docs/models/fable-5-1-field-guide) at the top of the stack, not a cheaper Sonnet; OpenAI's is [GPT-6 Astra](/docs/models/openai-astra-preview).

## Three things about this launch that surprise people

### 1. Parity with Opus 5 on two headline benchmarks, a 33-point gap on the third

Google's evaluation report, the rows a Claude shop should care about (higher is better):

| Benchmark | 3.8 Flash | 3.7 Flash | Claude Opus 5 | Claude Sonnet 5 | GPT-5.6 Sol |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **DeepSWE v1.1** (real bug fixes) | **73.7%** | 65.3% | 74.0% | 53.8% | 72.7% |
| **Terminal-bench 2.1** | **89.4%** | — | 89.1% | — | 88.8% |
| **Terminal-bench 4.0** | 19.1% | 11.2% | **51.8%** | 12.4% | 37.3% |
| **OSWorld-2.0** (computer use) | 59.0% | 50.6% | **75.4%** | 42.6% | 62.6% |
| **HLE-Verified** | **54.9%** | 53.6% | 54.4% | 31.0% | 54.5% |
| **GDPval-AA v2** (Elo) | 1545 | 1482 | **1824** | 1584 | 1710 |
| **Vals Finance Agent v2** | **61.4%** | 59.0% | 58.6% | 53.9% | 53.8% |
| **Harvey Legal Agent** | **10.0%** | 8.8% | 6.7% | 5.0% | 2.5% |
| **BioMysteryBench (hard)** | **56.5%** | 43.5% | 49.4% | 34.1% | 44.7% |

Three reads. First, the **DeepSWE +8.4** is the load-bearing number, exactly as the +16.3 was for 3.7 Flash: Google keeps pouring the workhorse's gains into repository-scale coding. Second, **Terminal-bench 2.1 vs 4.0** is the same split we flag on [DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash](/docs/models/deepseek-v4-1-flash-open-weight-1m) the following week: a cheap model can saturate the older suite while the harder one, with longer adaptive shell sessions, still separates Opus 5 by more than 30 points. Third, on the **domain-agent benchmarks** (finance, legal, biology) 3.8 Flash now leads every column, including Opus 5. If your work is a finance or legal agent, that row is worth an eval on its own. If your work is an interactive coding session, the 4.0 row is the one to budget around.

### 2. "Works harder" is a token-usage decision Google made for you

Google's own description of the change: on complex tasks the model "exhibits greater diligence — executing extra reasoning steps, and calling tools iteratively." The launch post also says developers can "utilize lower effort levels to minimize token overhead," which is a polite way of saying the default now spends more. Emergent's read of the evaluation report puts it at **roughly 30% more output tokens per task**; Artificial Analysis measured **$0.58 per Intelligence Index task, about 40% more than 3.7 Flash**, at the *same* per-token sticker.

For a Claude user this is the familiar `effort` trade-off wearing a different name: the model reaches for more tool calls and more thinking before it answers. The sticker did not move; the bill per task did. Budget on task cost, not token price. And re-run any latency SLO you had on 3.7 Flash: more iterations means more wall-clock, even at Flash speed. The physics is on [Why agents burn tokens](/docs/models/why-agents-burn-tokens).

### 3. Still cheap next to Claude, but the ratio depends on which Claude

Artificial Analysis puts 3.8 Flash at **59 on its Intelligence Index** (at `high` reasoning), tied with GPT-5.6 Sol at extra-high and Grok 4.6 at medium, versus **Claude Opus 5 at 63 and Claude Fable 5.1 at 66** (both at max). Its cost-per-task figure for Fable 5.1 is about **$3.76**, roughly **6×** 3.8 Flash's $0.58. Against Opus 5 on list price, Vellum's arithmetic is **about 6.7× cheaper on both input and output**. Against Sonnet 5 the gap is far smaller, and Sonnet 5 still leads on GDPval-AA v2 (1584 vs 1545). So the honest comparison is: 3.8 Flash is a Sonnet-tier price with DeepSWE and domain-agent scores that now reach Opus territory, and Terminal-bench 4.0 / OSWorld scores that do not. Current Claude list prices are on [Models and pricing](/docs/whats-new/models-and-pricing).

## Migrating from 3.7 Flash (or from Claude)

The API surface is unchanged from 3.7 Flash. The list of things that break a *Claude* port is also unchanged, and it keeps catching people.

## Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber and the Fairwind Program

Alongside the general model, Google released **Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber**, replacing 3.5 Flash Cyber. Google's numbers: **CWE-Bench patching at 47.2% pass@1** against "a leading frontier model" at 47.8%; a **vulnerability-discovery success rate above 70%** across 20 programming languages; **2.6× more correct patches to Chrome vulnerabilities** than the best commercial models per Chrome Security; and, per Wiz, **7.5–9.7 points higher recall at 2.3–5.2× lower cost**.

You almost certainly cannot call it. Access runs through the **Fairwind Program**, limited to "trusted government authorities, as well as critical infrastructure operators and software maintainers," by application. This is the same shape as OpenAI gating GPT-6 Astra's exploit capability behind a vetted program (see [the Astra field guide](/docs/models/openai-astra-preview)) and Anthropic's treatment of Mythos-class models. For a Claude user the practical takeaway is that the frontier's cyber capability now ships as a separate, gated SKU on all three stacks, and the general models are deliberately not it. Our [security section](/docs/security/prompt-injection) covers what that means for the agents you can call.
