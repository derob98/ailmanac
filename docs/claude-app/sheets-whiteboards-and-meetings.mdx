# Claude Sheets, Whiteboards & Meetings

On **November 10, 2026** Claude stopped being a chat window that *produces* work files and started being a place where the work file lives. Four things landed at once:

- **Claude Sheets** — a real spreadsheet inside Claude, in beta.
- **Claude Whiteboards** — a shared visual board, in beta.
- **Claude Meetings** — Claude as a named participant in a Google Meet call.
- **Claude Data** — the Dashboards beta, renamed and generally available.

Every outlet covered what these *are* within hours. Almost nobody wrote down what they **aren't** — and the gap list is where every real decision lives, because it determines whether you can move a workflow into Claude or have to keep it in Excel.

## The plan matrix

| Surface | Status | Plans | Enterprise default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Claude Sheets** | Beta | Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, Enterprise | **Off** until an owner enables it |
| **Claude Whiteboards** | Beta | Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, Enterprise | **Off** until an owner enables it |
| **Claude Data** | **GA** (was Dashboards beta) | All paid plans | On by default from **November 19, 2026** |
| **Claude Meetings** | Beta / announced — see note above | Team, Enterprise | Admin-gated |

Two things that catch people out:

**Free plans get none of it.** Sheets and Whiteboards start at Pro. This is a sharper line than the one drawn on October 8, when Docs, Slides and Design *left* beta and became available on every plan including Free.

**On Enterprise, these are off until somebody turns them on.** Team plans get Sheets and Whiteboards enabled by default; Enterprise does not. If you are an Enterprise user reading launch coverage and seeing no Sheets tile in your Artifacts tab, nothing is broken — an owner has to enable it in Organization settings. Whiteboards is also **not available in Claude for Teachers** at all.

## Claude Sheets: the real shape of it

The thing that distinguishes Sheets from "Claude generates an .xlsx for you" is that **Claude edits cells in the same sheet you are in**, outlining each change as it makes it, and the sheet is a shareable artifact your team can open and co-edit. It is a live surface, not a download.

You get it two ways: ask in any chat ("build me a campaign tracker"), or open the **Artifacts tab** and pick the Sheets tile — either describing what you want or starting blank.

### What's in the beta

- **144 functions.** Not "Excel's function library." A hundred and forty-four.
- Formulas you write yourself, plus direct cell editing — this is a spreadsheet, not a prompt box.
- **Conditional formatting**, dropdowns, checkboxes.
- Charts: bar, line, pie and a range of other types.
- Multiple tabs.
- **Cell comments** and simultaneous multi-user editing.
- **Import** from CSV, Google Sheets and Excel. **Export** to .xlsx or CSV, or "Send to" a partner app.
- Version history with restore.

### The hard ceiling

**A sheet holds up to 100,000 cells.**

That number deserves a moment, because spreadsheet people read "100,000" as "fine" and it often isn't. 100,000 cells is a **20-column table with 5,000 rows**. One year of daily data across 20 metrics is 7,300 cells — comfortable. One month of per-transaction exports from a mid-size store is not. And the cap is on *cells*, so empty-looking columns you formatted across still count against you.

Before you move a workflow in, do the arithmetic: columns × rows, per tab, summed.

### What is not supported

This list is the actual decision criterion, so here it is in full. Missing from the beta:

| Category | Not available |
| --- | --- |
| **Data manipulation** | Pivot tables, **find and replace**, multi-column sort, filter views, multi-select dropdowns |
| **Governance** | Protected ranges |
| **Export** | PDF export |
| **Excel power features** | Macros, VBA, Power Query, DAX, print layout |
| **Data freshness** | **Sheets can't pull live data from connected apps** |
| **Mobile** | Basic **read-only** access only |

Three of those are worth calling out specifically.

**No find and replace.** That is not an advanced feature, it is `Ctrl+H`. Its absence tells you honestly where the beta is: this is a spreadsheet you build and reason over with Claude, not one you do bulk mechanical surgery in. The intended answer is to *ask Claude* to make the change — which works, and is slower and less deterministic than the keystroke.

**No protected ranges.** Combined with multi-user editing and an agent that writes cells, you have a shared document where nothing is lockable. For a planning tracker that's fine. For a sheet that feeds a decision, assume any cell can change.

**No live data from connected apps.** See the next section — this is the single most important architectural fact about Sheets.

## Sheets and Claude Data have opposite data models

These two shipped on the same day and are easy to conflate. They are built on opposite assumptions, and picking the wrong one is the most expensive mistake available here.

| | **Claude Sheets** | **Claude Data** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the numbers live | **In the sheet.** Typed, imported, or written by Claude | **In your warehouse or CRM.** Claude queries it |
| Freshness | A **snapshot**. Cannot pull live data from connected apps | **Live**, with caching and a configurable refresh rate |
| Who can trust it | Whoever was in the room | Your data team, via **certified query definitions** |
| Edit model | Anyone edits any cell | Query-defined; viewers can be scoped to specific queries |
| Good for | Trackers, budgets, plans, one-off analysis, scratch models | Reporting, metrics everyone must agree on, data storytelling |

The giveaway is the **certified queries** feature in Claude Data: your data team can certify the queries everyone should use, specifically so that two dashboards don't disagree about the same number. That is a governance mechanism. Sheets has no equivalent, by design — it is a document, and documents drift.

So: if the number needs to be the same number tomorrow and for everyone, it belongs in **Claude Data**. If you are figuring something out, it belongs in **Sheets**. Our [Claude Dashboards & Claude Motion](/docs/claude-app/dashboards-and-motion) page covers the warehouse side in depth — note that the "Dashboards" name in it is now **Claude Data**, and the beta plan restrictions described there were lifted at GA.

## The compliance gap

For Sheets, Anthropic states that **HIPAA, CMEK and ZDR are not yet supported** — a beta limitation. Whiteboards carries the matching restriction: unavailable for HIPAA-ready organizations, and not covered by zero-data-retention configurations.

Read that plainly. The organizations with the most spreadsheet-shaped pain — hospitals, clinics, insurers, anyone under a BAA — and the security-conscious enterprises that negotiated zero data retention are precisely the ones that **cannot use these two surfaces yet**. If your org is HIPAA-ready or on ZDR, the tile may not appear, and if it does, using it would sit outside the agreement you think you are operating under. Confirm with whoever owns that contract before putting real data in.

## Claude Whiteboards: a thinking surface, deliberately walled off

A whiteboard is an artifact you, your teammates and Claude work on together. You **dictate a brain dump** into the microphone, or type, or sketch — and Claude groups related ideas, orders the steps, connects the dependencies, and draws it as shapes and arrows. Dictation supports **25 languages** including English, French, German, Spanish, Chinese, Japanese and Korean.

When the structure settles, Claude turns the board into a doc or a deck.

The two limits that shape how you use it:

**You cannot import into a whiteboard.** No existing diagram, no file. It starts empty and grows from what's said on it. Export is PNG or a send-to-partner handoff; there is no round trip.

**Claude cannot see your connected apps from a whiteboard.** It works only from the board's own content. So the whiteboard is a closed room: no warehouse, no Jira, no Drive. That is a real constraint and arguably the right one for a brainstorming surface — but it means "pull last quarter's numbers onto the board" is a thing you do by hand.

Whiteboards runs on **web, Claude Desktop and Claude Mobile**, and consumes your normal plan usage limits like any other Claude feature.

## Claude Meetings: the auto-leave rule

Claude joins a **Google Meet** call as a visible participant — support for other platforms is described as following later. During the call it can draft the plan being discussed, lay a timeline out on a whiteboard, answer questions in the meeting chat, and reach into connected work apps to fetch things like last year's figures.

The design detail worth knowing before you ever use it:

<Callout type="key" items={[
  "Claude identifies itself as a participant and posts a notice in the meeting chat — it is never a silent listener.",
  "It will not join calls with external guests unless an admin has authorized that.",
  "If an external guest joins a call Claude is already in, Claude automatically leaves."
]} />

That third rule is the one that will surprise a team in practice. You are 25 minutes into an internal planning call with Claude building the plan live, a client or contractor joins, and **Claude exits on its own.** The default is privacy-preserving and defensible — but if you are running a call that external people may drop into, do not make Claude's presence load-bearing for the output. Get the artifact saved before the room's membership can change.

## Interactive connectors: a different mechanism, not a longer list

Shipped alongside the above and easy to mis-file. The [connectors](/docs/claude-app/connectors) you already have are built on the Model Context Protocol: Claude **retrieves context** from an app. **Interactive connectors** embed the partner application **inside Claude's interface**, so you work in it without switching tabs — and they ship with built-in skills, so a seller can prep for a call or an accountant can work through financials in place.

Named at launch: **Adobe, Atlassian, NetSuite**, plus **Salesforce** and **Workday** in beta. **Claude auto-connect** is in beta on eligible Team and Enterprise plans.

The distinction matters when you're deciding where a workflow lives: a retrieval connector means Claude reads your app; an interactive connector means your app is now a panel in Claude.

Two reasons that prompt is shaped that way. Naming the decision the sheet supports is what turns a generic table into the right columns — Anthropic's own guidance is to say what the sheet is *for* and what you want to track. And stating the beta's constraints up front stops Claude from proposing a pivot-table-shaped summary you then have to undo.
