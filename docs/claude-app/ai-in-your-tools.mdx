You don't always go *to* an AI — increasingly it shows up **inside the apps you already work in**. Meeting Claude where you are removes the copy-paste tax and fits AI into real workflows.

## Common places AI shows up

- **Productivity suites** — assistants embedded in documents, email, spreadsheets, and slides (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace). Draft, summarize, and transform content without leaving the file.
- **Chat/collaboration** — assistants and bots in tools like Slack for summarizing threads, drafting replies, and answering from your knowledge base.
- **Browsers** — read and act on the current page — see [Claude in Chrome & Computer Use](/docs/claude-app/chrome-and-computer-use).
- **Your own apps** — via [Connectors (MCP)](/docs/claude-app/connectors) and the [API](/docs/api/first-call).

## Two ways to "bring AI to your tools"

1. **Use an existing integration** — install the official app/add-in/bot where it exists. Fastest path.
2. **Connect via MCP** — [Connectors](/docs/claude-app/connectors) let Claude reach external tools/data through the open [Model Context Protocol](/docs/claude-code/mcp), so it can work *with* your systems.

:::tip[Which one first?]
If an official integration exists for the tool, start there — you get it working today. Reach for [MCP](/docs/claude-code/mcp) when the system is yours, niche, or internal, or when you need Claude to combine several systems in one task.
:::

## Make it useful (and safe)

<Steps items={[
  {
    title: "Start with read and summarize",
    body: "Summarizing a thread or a document is low risk and immediately useful. It also tells you whether the integration actually has the access you think it has."
  },
  {
    title: "Grant least privilege, deliberately",
    body: "An integration that can act on your accounts inherits real power. Scope it to what the task needs and no more — see [Securing Agents & Tools](/docs/security/securing-agents)."
  },
  {
    title: "Assume everything it reads is untrusted",
    body: "Emails, shared docs, and web pages can contain text aimed at the assistant rather than at you. That's [prompt injection](/docs/security/prompt-injection), and an integration with write access is exactly the target."
  },
  {
    title: "Check the data terms before work data goes in",
    body: "Different surfaces and plans treat your content differently. Confirm what's retained and what's used for training — see [Privacy](/docs/foundations/privacy)."
  },
  {
    title: "Only then let it take actions",
    body: "Sending, posting, filing, or editing on your behalf is the last step, not the first — and it deserves a narrower permission scope than reading did."
  }
]} />
